        About this plotBuilt on the fly from every exposure: the target is measured on the original detector pixels at its predicted position, with the IRSA SPHEREx Source Discovery Tool method (3-pixel aperture, ePSF-corrected). Points whose aperture holds a flagged pixel are hidden unless you switch them on. White diamonds: 2MASS J, H, Ks. Orange squares: WISE W1, W2 (CatWISE2020), matched at each catalogue's own date.
        About this plotBrightness over time with the spectrum taken out: each clean measurement is divided by the median of the same target in the same SPHEREx channel. A steady star sits on 1; a variable star (R Draconis, RW Aurigae) moves away from 1.

        Shift-and-add
        What it doesSlide every exposure along a chosen motion before stacking (as in WiseView): an object moving that way stays still and builds up into a sharp dot, everything else smears. Use it to confirm a faint candidate.

        Report a candidate

        Skytrace writes up what you are looking at: the link to this exact view, your pinned position, the nearest catalogued stars, and the SPHEREx exposures in the frame. Add your notes, then copy or download it to share with a teammate, a teacher, or a citizen-science community such as Backyard Worlds: Planet 9.

        Before you report, check:
        • It shows in two or more visits (step through the frames), not just one.
        • The pinned pixel is not flagged as saturated, masked or an artefact (shown in the report).
        • It is not a known star: the report lists the nearest Gaia DR3 and CatWISE2020 sources and their known motion.
        • For a moving dot, try Shift-and-add with its motion: a real mover becomes sharper.
